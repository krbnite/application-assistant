## Letter

Dear Hiring Team,

I am applying for the Data Scientist position in New York because I want to bring physical simulation and machine learning closer together in my next role. PhysicsX's work alongside simulation engineers and customers makes that connection concrete: the model has to help someone make an engineering decision.

My simulation work began with translating analytical granular-dynamics models into numerical simulations and visualizations, contributing to four peer-reviewed publications. In a later independent collaboration, I worked with mechanical-engineering researchers on RNN forecasting and PyTorch surrogate-modeling approaches for expensive tapped-granular-system simulations. At Podimetrics, I developed synthetic thermograms and Newtonian heat-transfer models to extrapolate short scans toward thermal equilibrium.

At Podimetrics, I owned the production thermal-imaging, modeling, and detection pipeline and substantially refactored its Python code. I worked with hardware and software engineers to adapt processing and calibration models for SmartMat+, enabling the second-generation device's release. In separate fall-risk work, I developed adaptive estimators that reduced the time to reliable patient-specific estimates from roughly 20–30 days to 3–5 days, translated the selected estimator from Python to C, and verified numerical equivalence for firmware integration.

At Cohen Veterans Bioscience, I built SensorNet to replace repeated biosignal-model implementations with a configurable internal package. I also redesigned a gesture-recognition evaluation after finding leakage and time-ordering bias. That work required asking whether an apparent model improvement would hold for an unseen patient—a question that changed how the experiment needed to be run. Across my work, I have explained modeling choices and limits to engineering, clinical, and executive partners.

I would welcome the opportunity to bring that combination of physical modeling, software development, and experimental judgment to the Delivery team, working with engineers and customers to turn models into useful tools.

Sincerely,
Kevin Urban, PhD

## Editorial notes

- **Central argument:** Physical modeling connected to implementation and engineering decisions.
- **Why it works:** Specific simulation motivation leads into actual analytical/numerical work, independent collaboration, production pipeline ownership, hardware delivery and stakeholder communication.
- **Limits:** The surrogate work is an independent collaboration, not a demonstrated industrial solver replacement. Domain-specific tooling and customer scope may require different evidence.
